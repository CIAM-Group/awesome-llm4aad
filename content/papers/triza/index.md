## Why it matters

A better detector score need not justify replacing an incumbent when other benefits, harms, or validation requirements deteriorate.

## Core method

TRIZa maps inventive principles to typed code or configuration transformations. Before evaluation, each proposal declares its contradiction, operator, allowed changes, and frozen protocol. The LLM instantiates the edit; a provenance record links it to the resulting evidence.

Useful and harmful functional information are measured separately against fixed reference populations. Integrity, safety, reproducibility, and resource gates determine eligibility before certification level and replication status are compared. FI-based Ideality is diagnostic or tie-breaking evidence, not permission to override a failed gate.

## Contributions

Paired legacy reruns report F1 gains on three cybersecurity datasets. More importantly, a completed 45-activity CICIoT2023 campaign improves macro-F1 from 0.8325 to 0.8483 but is not promoted: both the relative-gain and false-positive-reduction thresholds fail. Later deteriorating candidates are also retained as negative evidence.

## Strengths and limitations

Frozen protocols and separate decision levels make score improvements auditable. They do not establish that TRIZ or FI caused the gains: matched-budget component ablations are absent. The 2,040 controller decisions form a nested descriptive aggregate, not independent replications. Raw trace artifacts are promised for later release, so current reproducibility is manuscript-level.

## What to improve

Release complete records, validate reference-population sensitivity, and compare typed versus unguided edits with identical budgets and promotion rules.

## Connections

[AlphaEvolve](../alphaevolve/index.md) evaluates generated variants through task scores. TRIZa takes a concrete alternative route by constraining mutation types before execution and separating useful metric change from evidence-qualified promotion.
